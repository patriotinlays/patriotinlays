# Questions, answered simply

If you're new to this, you're in the right place. These are the questions people tend to have before they start, answered the way we'd answer them across a kitchen table, with a link to the official source behind each one. If your question isn't here, the [Contact](/contact/) page reaches the site's owner.

## Am I eligible to join?

DAR is open to women who can prove they descend from an ancestor who aided in achieving American independence. [[claim:dar-join-eligible]] SAR is open to men who can prove they descend from a patriot of the American Revolution. [[claim:sar-about-age]] [[claim:sar-about-eligible]] The word "prove" is doing the work in both sentences: the society's own genealogists review what you send, not this site. [[claim:dar-review-staff]]

## What exactly is a patriot?

Someone who supported American independence. That can mean fighting in its battles or serving in the Continental Congress, but it isn't only soldiers. [[claim:sar-about-patriots]] DAR also counts people who furnished supplies, lent money or provided munitions. [[claim:dar-material-aid]] If your ancestor never carried a musket, he or she may still count, and our [guide to patriotic service](/guides/patriotic-service/) walks through those other kinds of help.

## What if I don't know my patriot's name?

That's where most people start, and you don't have to solve it before touching anything else. Start with yourself and work back one generation at a time, writing down what you already know. DAR's own first research step is the same: gather what you already know about your family history. [[claim:dar-join-gather]] [Step 0 of the Roadmap guide](/guides/lineage-proof-roadmap/#step-0-find-the-soldier-s-name) shows how to find the soldier's name, and the [worksheet](/tools/lineage-matrix/) lets you record the people you do know while you look.

## What records will I need?

Both societies ask you to document the line from you back to a patriot. DAR asks for every name, date, place and relationship [[claim:dar-each-relationship]], with complete birth and death dates and places for generations 1–3 [[claim:dar-gen-1-3]], and at least one documented date and place of birth or death for older generations who lived before modern vital records [[claim:dar-gen-older]]. SAR asks for proof of bloodline descent from the patriot to the applicant [[claim:sar-bloodline]], and two current SAR members sponsor the applicant [[claim:sar-sponsors]]; it does not take family tradition about service as proof [[claim:sar-tradition]]. The [side-by-side table in the Roadmap](/guides/lineage-proof-roadmap/#what-each-generation-needs-dar-and-sar-side-by-side) lays out both. The [worksheet](/tools/lineage-matrix/) turns that into a to-do list for your own family.

## What if a birth or death certificate doesn't exist?

You're not stuck, and you don't need to invent one. For people who lived before modern vital records, DAR asks for at least one documented date and place of birth or death, rather than a certificate by that name. [[claim:dar-gen-older]] A family genealogy may be acceptable for a specific person or generation if it meets DAR's standards. [[claim:dar-genealogy-may]] Our [substitute-evidence guide](/guides/substitute-evidence/) goes through what to try instead, and the worksheet shows those substitutes right beside the gap.

## Can I use a family story or an old family tree as proof?

Family stories are where you start looking, not where you stop. SAR does not take family tradition as proof of service. [[claim:sar-tradition]] A family genealogy may be acceptable for a particular person or generation if it meets DAR's standards, so a tree can point you to records even when it can't stand alone. [[claim:dar-genealogy-may]] And when two records disagree, the worksheet lets you mark the disagreement rather than quietly picking the date you prefer.

## Do I have to hire a genealogist?

No. A DAR member helps guide you through completing and submitting the application. [[claim:dar-member-guides]] SAR's local sponsor can guide you through the application. [[claim:sar-registrar-guide]] That help is part of joining, and our guides are here to make your conversations with that person easier.

## What does this site cost, and do I need an account?

The guides and both tools are free, and there is no account to create. What you type into the tools stays in your own browser on the device you're using, so there's nothing uploaded to a server somewhere. The [Privacy page](/privacy/) explains exactly what's saved and how to clear it.

## Does this site search records for me, or decide whether I qualify?

Neither. The tools organize what you already have and compare dates with published rules. The societies decide membership, and only their genealogists review your records. [[claim:dar-review-staff]] This site also isn't affiliated with DAR, SAR or any lineage society, and it isn't professional genealogical advice.

## How can I trust what's written here?

Every factual statement is tied to a quoted passage from an official or institutional source, and when the site is built, software checks that each quote is still there. If a statement can't be matched to a source, it's left out. The sources are listed at the bottom of each page, and the [Sources & Methodology page](/sources/) explains the method. Written with AI assistance and reviewed by the site's owner.

## What do I do next?

If you're starting from nothing, begin with the [Roadmap guide](/guides/lineage-proof-roadmap/). If you already have a pile of records, the [Lineage Evidence Matrix](/tools/lineage-matrix/) will sort them into a to-do list. If a widow's pension file might help, try the [Widow Pension Law Checker](/tools/widow-pension-checker/). And if you'd like to see either tool in action before typing anything, the [walkthroughs](/walkthroughs/) use a made-up family to show exactly what you'll see.
