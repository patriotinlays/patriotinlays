# The Patriot Lineage Proof Roadmap: Proving Every Generation for DAR or SAR

::in-short
This guide helps you: plan the chain of generations from you back to your patriot before you start filling in forms.
Start by: writing the chain first: every generation from you back to the patriot, names only.
Then: build an evidence grid for each generation in the [Lineage Evidence Matrix](/tools/lineage-matrix/).
::end

A lineage application is a chain. Each link is one generation, and each link holds only if the documents behind it hold. This roadmap shows how to plan that chain before you start filling in forms, using the published rules of the two largest Revolutionary War lineage societies. It is a planning aid, not a ruling: only the society's genealogists decide whether a line is approved.

## Step 0: find your patriot's name

Everything else in this guide depends on knowing which ancestor was the patriot. If you don't know your patriot's name yet, start here.

- **Start with what you know.** DAR's first research step is to ask what you already know about your family history and lineage. [[claim:dar-join-gather]]
- **Search DAR's free Genealogical Research System.** DAR offers it free to help with research and with the membership process; it is linked from [DAR's How to Join page](https://www.dar.org/discover/become-member/how-join). [[claim:dar-join-grs]] [[claim:dar-grs-purpose]]
- **Search the pension papers.** [DAR's index to Revolutionary War pension papers](https://services.dar.org/Public/DAR_Research/search_revwar/?Tab_ID=14&Tab_ID_Sub=0&action=list) lists the pensioner or widow and also other people named in the papers. [[claim:prp-pension-index]] The [National Archives says](https://www.archives.gov/research/military/american-revolution) the pension files are online free on FamilySearch (login needed). [[claim:nara-amrev-familysearch]]
- **Treat a family story as a lead, not proof.** SAR does not accept family tradition about an ancestor's service as proof. [[claim:sar-tradition]]
- **Stuck?** DAR's Membership Interest Form connects you to a chapter. [[claim:dar-join-interest]]

### Where to search for your patriot by name

Work down this list. A name that doesn't appear in one place isn't a dead end.

1. **[DAR Ancestor Search](https://services.dar.org/Public/DAR_Research/search/?Tab_ID=1) — free.** It searches the patriots DAR has already established in its GRS, and the GRS lists only patriots someone has joined DAR through or had a supplemental application verified, so a missing name doesn't mean your ancestor didn't serve. [[claim:dar-ancestor-search]] [[claim:dar-grs-only-joined]]
2. **[SAR Patriot Research System](https://sarpatriots.sar.org/).** A useful second check, but SAR says it isn't a comprehensive list of everyone who served and isn't acceptable as proof of service for an application. [[claim:sar-prs-not-complete]] [[claim:sar-prs-not-proof]]
3. **[FamilySearch pension index](https://www.familysearch.org/search/collection/1417475): free with a FamilySearch login.** Search it for a federal pension or bounty-land file. [[claim:nara-amrev-fs-m804]]
4. **[The National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/research/military/american-revolution) — free to search; copies cost money.** You can search within the pension series for a name in its online catalog, and order copies of pension and bounty-land applications with NATF Form 85 or online. [[claim:nara-mission-search-within]] [[claim:nara-pensions-natf85-online]] It does not hold state militia records, so for those, see your state's guidance below. [[claim:nara-gen-nomilitia]]

If none of these find your patriot, check your state's archives for militia records.

Once you have a name, the free [Lineage Evidence Matrix](/tools/lineage-matrix/) starts with your patriot: state, type of service and rough dates.

## If your patriot is already in DAR's or SAR's database

If DAR has already accepted your patriot, an earlier member's approved application can help with your research and can also be used as documentation for your own application. [[claim:dar-lib-record-copy-use]]
DAR calls these copies "record copies," and you can download them online. [[claim:dar-linres-record-copies]]
Older applications may include little information, so check what each one actually proves. [[claim:dar-linres-little-info]]
SAR also offers record copies of approved applications. [[claim:sar-record-copy]]
Ask your chapter registrar which of your generations still need proof.

## How the generations are numbered

On a DAR application the lineage starts with you, the applicant, as generation 1. [[claim:dar-gen1-applicant]]
Your parents are generation 2, your grandparents generation 3, and your great-grandparents generation 4. [[claim:dar-gen-numbering]]
DAR suggests numbering the generations on a separate pedigree chart or outline so you stay on track. [[claim:dar-pedigree]]
SAR asks for the patriot to be a great-great-grandparent of some degree, so every SAR line runs at least five generations from the applicant. [[claim:sar-ggp]]

The chain follows biology. DAR lineages list the biological parents in each generation. [[claim:dar-biological]]
Step-parents and adoptive parents are left off the DAR form. [[claim:dar-no-step]]
SAR states that it requires proof of bloodline descent, whether legitimate or illegitimate. [[claim:sar-bloodline]]

## What each generation needs: DAR and SAR side by side

| Question | DAR | SAR |
|---|---|---|
| Who counts as the patriot? | Military, civil or patriotic service between 19 April 1775 and 26 November 1783, with a few special windows [[claim:dar-window-core]] | Patriotic service, with some exceptions, between 19 April 1775 and 26 November 1783 [[claim:sar-window]] |
| Generations 1–3 | Complete dates and places of birth and death for each person [[claim:dar-gen-1-3]] | Not stated |
| Older generations | At least one documented date and place of birth or death for people who lived before modern vital records [[claim:dar-gen-older]] | Proof of bloodline descent from the patriot to the applicant [[claim:sar-bloodline]] |
| Both parents? | Both the man and the woman in each generation are documented [[claim:dar-both-parents]] | Not stated |
| What has to be proven? | Every name, date, place and relationship [[claim:dar-each-relationship]] | Family tradition about service is not proof [[claim:sar-tradition]] |
| Sponsors | Not stated | Two current SAR members sponsor the applicant [[claim:sar-sponsors]] |

Not stated means the society's own pages don't say; ask its registrar.

## The roadmap, step by step

1. Write the chain first, evidence second. List every generation from you back to the patriot by number, with names only. Mark each link you believe but cannot yet show on paper.
2. Check whether the patriot is already on file. {{asof:dar-eapp-established}} DAR says its electronic application works only for ancestors already fully established with DAR. [[claim:dar-eapp-established]] A new ancestor, or a line that needs an analysis compiling indirect evidence, goes on the traditional paper application. [[claim:dar-paper-analysis]]
3. Build an evidence grid. For every generation, list birth, marriage, death and the parent-child link, and next to each one the record that supports it. The [Lineage Evidence Matrix](/tools/lineage-matrix/) does this in your browser.
4. Use the record types reviewers see most often. DAR names vital records, census records, probate records, court records, land records and church records as the most common lineage sources. [[claim:dar-lineage-sources]]
5. Replace trees with records. DAR states that online family trees from any website are not an acceptable form of documentation. [[claim:dar-online-trees]] Images of original records downloaded from a website are a different matter and are evaluated in the context of the whole lineage. [[claim:dar-images-ok]]
6. Prefer original records over compiled books. DAR prefers primary and original records over compiled family genealogies. [[claim:dar-primary-preferred]]
7. Cite as you go. For a census, DAR asks for the year, state, county, town, enumeration district and page number. [[claim:dar-census-cite]] {{asof:dar-upload-each}} DAR says every uploaded document is cited at least once, and every cited document is uploaded. [[claim:dar-upload-each]]
8. Prove the patriot's service separately from the lineage. DAR lists pensions, muster rolls, pay vouchers, oaths of allegiance, town meeting minutes and county court orders as typical service sources. [[claim:dar-service-sources]]

## Practical details people miss

{{asof:dar-jpg}} DAR says e-application uploads are JPG files only, so convert PDFs and phone scans before you start. [[claim:dar-jpg]]
{{asof:dar-4digit}} DAR says years are entered as four digits. [[claim:dar-4digit]]
{{asof:dar-six-weeks}} DAR says staff review generally takes about six weeks once an application is submitted. [[claim:dar-six-weeks]]
{{asof:dar-fee}} DAR says the application cost includes a $75 application fee plus the first year's national dues, and chapters and state societies add their own dues. [[claim:dar-fee]]
