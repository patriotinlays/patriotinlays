# Patriot or Loyalist? How to Determine Which Side Your Ancestor Took

Families changed sides, neighbors split, and some people tried to stay out of it altogether. A lineage society cares about which side your ancestor ended up on, not only about one act of service. This guide shows how to test both sides of the record before you rely on a patriot claim.

## Why it matters for an application

SAR's bylaws describe an eligible ancestor as one who was at all times unfailing in loyalty to, and rendered acceptable service in, the cause of American independence. [[claim:sar-loyalty]]
DAR reviews service when a patriot's residence is a known center of Loyalist activity. [[claim:darblog-loyalist]]
In those cases DAR checks that the service supported the American cause and that it was the ancestor's last known act. [[claim:darblog-last-act]]
The best-known center DAR names is Long Island, New York. [[claim:darblog-longisland]]
Martha's Vineyard and Nantucket signed neutrality agreements to support neither side. [[claim:darblog-neutral]]

## A real case of a switched side

The National Park Service describes William Shoemaker, who testified in his pension claim that he was wounded and captured fighting for the Americans. A historian later found he had been captured earlier and subsequently enlisted in a New Jersey Loyalist battalion. [[claim:nps-shoemaker]]
His deception went unnoticed by the Pension Office clerks. [[claim:nps-shoemaker-approved]]
An approved pension is strong evidence, but it is not a guarantee about the whole war.

## Search both sides

1. Build the patriot timeline. List every dated act of service with its source and place, using the Service Window Checker to confirm each date sits in a published window.
2. Find the last known act. Sort the timeline by date and note the latest act, because DAR looks at whether pro-American service was the last known act. [[claim:darblog-last-act]]
3. Check the residence. If he lived in a known Loyalist center or a neutral area during the war, expect closer review.
4. Search Loyalist records under every spelling. The United Empire Loyalists' Association of Canada (UELAC) keeps a Loyalist Directory whose entries record first enlistment into the Loyalist military [[claim:uelac-enlistment]] and the place the Loyalist lived before being expelled. [[claim:uelac-settled-before]]
5. Read the status field. In UELAC's directory, "Proven" means its Dominion Genealogist has accepted the person as a Loyalist. [[claim:uelac-proven]] "Expunged" and "Suspended" mean removal from the early Loyalist lists, usually by Order in Council. [[claim:uelac-expunged]] "Disproven" means new information shows the person does not meet the criteria. [[claim:uelac-disproven]]
6. Separate same-name men. UELAC uses a digit to tell same-name Loyalists apart, which is a reminder that a Loyalist entry with your ancestor's name may be a different man. [[claim:uelac-digit]] See [Same Name, Different Man](/guides/same-name/).

## Reading the result

| What you find | What it suggests | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Patriot service only, no Loyalist record | No conflict found in the records checked | Document the service and residence |
| Loyalist entry for a man of the same name, different place | Possibly a different man | Compare places, ages and families |
| Loyalist entry for the same man, before his patriot service | A change of side toward the Patriots | Show that patriot service is the last known act |
| Loyalist entry for the same man, after his patriot service | A change of side toward the Crown | Expect the service to be questioned |
| Residence in a known Loyalist or neutral area | Closer review | Gather service records from the place he lived |

Two cautions. Loyalist status itself has shifted over time: individuals have been accepted and rejected as Loyalists at various times since 1789. [[claim:uelac-1789]]
And family stories do not settle the question either way: SAR does not take family tradition about service as proof. [[claim:sar-tradition]]

## Record it

Enter each pro- and anti-independence record in the Lineage Evidence Matrix against the patriot's generation and use the conflict flag where they disagree. The report page lists every flagged conflict first.
