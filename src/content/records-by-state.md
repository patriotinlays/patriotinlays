# Revolutionary War Records by State: Where Each Kind of Service Was Recorded

The most common dead end in Revolutionary War research is searching the right name in the wrong repository. Where a record sits depends mostly on who the ancestor served for. This guide gives you a decision procedure first and a list of starting points second.

## The one rule that decides most searches

The National Archives holds records of Continental troops and of state troops that served as Continental troops. [[claim:nara-svc-holds]]
It does not hold state militia records. [[claim:nara-svc-custody]]
For militia service, NARA's advice is to check with the archives of the state in which the soldier served. [[claim:nara-svc-state]]

## Decision procedure

1. Identify the type of service. Continental Army, state troops, militia, navy, or civil and patriotic service.
2. Continental, or state troops serving as Continentals: start with the federal compiled service records and rolls (below).
3. Militia: go to the state archives or state library of the state he served for, and check the pension files too, since the 1832 act opened pensions to militia service. [[claim:nps-1832]]
4. Civil or patriotic service, such as town office, committees, oaths or supplies: DAR lists oaths of allegiance, town meeting minutes and county court orders among service sources, so start with town and county records, then the state archives. [[claim:dar-service-sources]]
5. Unsure: search the federal pension files first. A veteran's application normally gives his former rank, unit and period of service, which tells you where to search next. [[claim:m804-rank-unit]]

When you record the result, DAR's service-state field means the state the ancestor served for. [[claim:dar-service-state]]
For military service it is the state in control of his unit, not every state he marched through. [[claim:dar-unit-state]]

## Federal records: what exists and what it covers

| Series | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| M881 | Compiled military service records, 1,096 rolls [[claim:nara-m881]] | In the National Archives Catalog as NAID 570910 [[claim:nara-amrev-m881]] |
| M860 | Name index to those compiled records, 58 rolls [[claim:nara-m860]] | Start here for a name search |
| M246 | Revolutionary War rolls, 138 rolls [[claim:nara-m246]] | In the Catalog as NAID 602384 [[claim:nara-amrev-m246]] |
| M920, M1051, M257 | Indexes for Connecticut [[claim:nara-m920]], Georgia [[claim:nara-m1051]] and North Carolina [[claim:nara-m257]] organizations | State-specific indexes |
| M804 | Pension and bounty-land application files [[claim:nara-amrev-m804]] | Includes militia veterans who applied under later acts |
| M910 | Virginia half-pay pension files [[claim:nara-m910]] | Virginia-specific |
| M829 | Bounty-land warrants used in the Ohio military district, acts of 1788, 1803 and 1806 [[claim:nara-m829]] | Land, not service, but often names the soldier |

Compiled service records were assembled in the 1890s from muster rolls, descriptive rolls and pay rolls. [[claim:nara-cmsr-1890s]]
They were made as substitutes for records destroyed by fires in 1800 and 1814. [[claim:nara-cmsr-fires]]
So a Continental soldier may have served and still have no compiled service record. [[claim:nara-cmsr-missing]]
To order copies, NARA uses NATF Form 86 for service records [[claim:nara-natf86]] and NATF Form 85 for pension and pre-1856 bounty-land files. [[claim:nara-natf85]]

## State and society repositories

The New York Public Library's guide lists examples of state repositories with Revolutionary War finding aids, including:

- Connecticut State Library, Revolutionary War Service Records [[claim:nypl-ct]]
- Delaware Public Archives, guide to Revolutionary War records [[claim:nypl-de]]
- Pennsylvania Historical and Museum Commission, Revolutionary War overview [[claim:nypl-pa]]
- South Carolina Department of Archives and History, sources for the American Revolution [[claim:nypl-sc]]

NYPL says its list is representative, not exhaustive. [[claim:nypl-representative]]
For other states it suggests searching for the State Archives or the State Historical Society. [[claim:nypl-search]]

DAR's Patriot Records Project index covers the 13 original states and some newer states, including Maine, Vermont and Ohio. [[claim:prp-coverage]]
It has indexed over 500,000 documents [[claim:prp-500k]] and includes some French and Spanish records. [[claim:prp-french-spanish]]
DAR's Revolutionary War Pension Index also names other people mentioned in pension papers, such as witnesses, which can surface service that appears only in someone else's file. [[claim:prp-pension-index]]

## One state example: Virginia

Virginia shows how state records add to the federal ones. After a 1779 act, Virginia offered its own bounty land for Revolutionary War service. [[claim:lva-1779]]
State bounty land went to soldiers or sailors with at least three years of continuous Continental or state service. [[claim:lva-3yrs]]
No state bounty land was given for militia service. [[claim:lva-militia]]
The Library of Virginia has put images of those state bounty-land application files and certificate files online. [[claim:lva-files]] The federal Virginia half-pay files are a separate NARA series. [[claim:nara-m910]]
