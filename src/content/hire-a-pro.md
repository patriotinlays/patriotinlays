# Should You Hire A Pro?

::in-short
This guide helps you: decide whether a paid genealogist is worth it, and find a qualified one if it is.
Start by: using the help the societies offer and this site's free tools to organize what you have.
Then: bring your [Lineage Evidence Matrix](/tools/lineage-matrix/) report to any volunteer or pro you work with.
::end

## The Short Answer

You may not need to pay anyone. Start with the help the societies offer, and use this site's free tools to organize what you have. A paid genealogist is a tool for a specific problem — and the more of your own work you bring, the less you're likely to pay.

## Start With The Societies' Own Help

- DAR: DAR says, "A member will help guide you through completing and submitting your DAR membership application." [[claim:dar-member-guides]]
- SAR: the chapter registrar is often your local contact, who guides you through the application and can review what you've gathered. [[claim:sar-sponsor-often-registrar]] [[claim:sar-registrar-guide]] SAR also says: "State SAR Societies have genealogical helpers who assist potential members with application preparation." [[claim:sar-rc-helpers]]
- Free tools on this site. The Lineage Evidence Matrix turns the records you have into a list of what's still missing for each generation and where to look.

Society volunteers help with the application; that's not the same as doing your research for you. Ask up front whether any help comes with a cost.

## Signs A Pro Might Be Worth It

- You've searched for one generation's link for months and can't connect it.
- The records you need are in a courthouse or archive far from home.
- Records for your county or era were burned, lost or never kept.
- You've found the records, but they disagree, or you can't tell whether they prove the parent–child link. Finding records and judging what they prove are different skills.
- You simply don't have the time, and you'd rather pay than wait.

## What A Pro Can And Can't Do

A pro can find, copy and interpret records you can't get to yourself, and help you work out which records will prove a link. A pro can't guarantee acceptance: the society decides what it will and won't accept as proof, "so there's never a guarantee." [[claim:ll-no-guarantee]]

## What It Costs

These are published examples, not standard prices. Rates vary by researcher and change over time.

- About $15: copies from the SAR Library start at a $15 donation for the first 25 pages. [[claim:sarlib-copies-15]]
- About $30–$75 an hour: research help from the societies themselves. SAR charges $30 an hour for members and $35 for non-members [[claim:sar-rc-rate]]; the DAR Library's search service charges $50 an hour for DAR members and $75 for non-members. [[claim:darlib-search-member]] [[claim:darlib-search-nonmember]]
- About $50–$200 an hour: private professional genealogists. A genealogy accrediting body says many charge between $50 and $200 per hour. [[claim:icapgen-50-200]]
- Thousands of dollars: full "done for you" application packages. One example from a different lineage society (not DAR or SAR): in its October 2024 price list, the Mayflower Society charged $2,500 for members and $3,250 for non-members for application preparation. [[claim:mayflower-app-prep]]

## How To Find A Qualified Pro

Three organizations keep directories, and they mean different things:

- Board for Certification of Genealogists (BCG): awards the Certified Genealogist credential; its directory lists Board-Certified Genealogists. [[claim:bcg-award]] [[claim:bcg-find]]
- International Commission for the Accreditation of Professional Genealogists (ICAPGen): its directory lists Accredited Genealogist professionals. [[claim:icapgen-directory]]
- Association of Professional Genealogists (APG): a professional organization representing more than 2,000 members, with a [searchable directory](https://www.apgen.org/directory). [[claim:apg-professional-org]] [[claim:apg-2000-members]] Membership is not the same as a credential.

A good genealogist isn't automatically familiar with DAR or SAR rules. Look for experience with lineage-society applications.

Questions to ask before you hire:

- Have you worked on DAR or SAR applications?
- Do you know the records in my patriot's state?
- Exactly what relationship or record will you investigate, and where will you search?
- What will I get at the end: copies of records, citations, notes, a written report?
- What will it cost, and is there a limit on hours?

Red flag: anyone who promises your application will be accepted.

## The Smart Order: Free First, Paid When Stuck

Start with the free resources available to you. Then, whenever possible, hire a professional to investigate a clearly defined research problem rather than building your whole line from scratch. A pro billing by the hour who starts from your finished generations and a clear list of what's missing has less ground to cover.

## Bring Your Work With You

Whether your helper is a society volunteer or a paid pro, bring:

- Your Lineage Evidence Matrix report. The worksheet makes a "What to do next" plan and report you can print or save as a PDF.
- Copies of the records listed in it.

Save your report before your meeting: the worksheet is saved only in your browser, so clearing your browser data or switching browsers deletes your work.

Remember what the report is: a to-do list, not proof. The worksheet's best label is "Evidence Entered — Review Required." Only the society decides whether your evidence meets its requirements. [[claim:ll-no-guarantee]]
