# Bounty Land Warrants: What They Prove, and What They Don't

::in-short
This guide helps you: sort out what a bounty-land file shows about service and family, and where it misleads.
Start by: checking the proof-value table to see what the file can and cannot show.
Then: log any mismatch between the land and the family's home in the [Lineage Evidence Matrix](/tools/lineage-matrix/).
::end

Bounty land was a promise of land in exchange for service. For a researcher, the files it created are evidence of service and, often, of family. They are weak evidence of where an ancestor lived, because many warrants were sold. This guide sorts out which is which.

## Federal bounty land in brief

In plain terms, a bounty-land warrant was a right to free land in the public domain, granted to veterans and their heirs for their service. [[claim:m804-bl-define]] [[claim:m804-bl-who]] After the war, these grants became a form of reward. [[claim:m804-bl-reward]]

A Continental Congress resolution of September 16, 1776 was the basic law for federal Revolutionary War bounty-land warrants until 1855. [[claim:m804-bl-1776]]
It gave 100 acres to each noncommissioned officer and soldier. [[claim:m804-bl-100]]
A 1780 resolution extended grants to generals: 850 acres for a brigadier general and 1,100 acres for a major general. [[claim:m804-bl-generals]]
The act of March 3, 1855 offered 160-acre warrants to veterans who served as few as 14 days or fought in any battle, and covered their widows and minor children. [[claim:nara-bounty-1855]]
An 1856 act extended that to naval and marine personnel. [[claim:m804-1856]]
Bounty-land files relate to wartime service between 1775 and March 3, 1855. [[claim:nara-gen-bl-range]]

## Proof-value table

| Question | What a bounty-land file can show | Reliability |
|---|---|---|
| Did your patriot serve? | An approved warrant rests on claimed service; Virginia certificates verify service [[claim:lva-certificates]] | Strong for service |
| Where did your patriot live? | The land named is not necessarily where your patriot lived: many recipients stayed home and sold the warrants [[claim:m804-sold]] | Weak for residence |
| Did your patriot own the land? | Virginia certificates were often sold or assigned to speculators [[claim:lva-assigned]] | Weak for ownership |
| Who was your patriot's family? | Files can hold applications by widows or heirs, Bible pages and marriage-record copies [[claim:m804-bl-contents]] | Strong when heirs applied |

A warrant holder could "locate" it, choosing public land in exchange. [[claim:m804-locate]]
Because many holders sold instead, a land entry far from home is not, on its own, evidence that the family moved. [[claim:m804-sold]]

## Where the files are

Many bounty-land files for Revolutionary War and War of 1812 service were combined with the pension files, so search M804 first. [[claim:nara-gen-combined]]
A separate unindexed series includes disapproved applications based on Revolutionary War service. [[claim:nara-gen-unindexed]]
On the envelope, B. L. Wt. marks a bounty-land file [[claim:m804-blwt]], and a trailing "55" marks a warrant under the 1855 act. [[claim:m804-55]]
Warrants used in the Ohio military district under the acts of 1788, 1803 and 1806 are a separate federal series, M829. [[claim:nara-m829]]
NARA orders pension and pre-1856 bounty-land files with NATF Form 85. [[claim:nara-natf85]]

## State bounty land: the Virginia example

States granted their own bounty land as well. Virginia began after a 1779 legislative act. [[claim:lva-1779]]
Its grants called for at least three years of continuous Continental or state service. [[claim:lva-3yrs]]
Militia service earned no Virginia state bounty land. [[claim:lva-militia]]
Grants ran from 100 acres for a soldier or sailor to 15,000 acres for a major general. [[claim:lva-acreage]]
Virginia issued 9,926 certificates between 1782 and 1876. [[claim:lva-9926]]
Heirs kept filing for decades, so a late claim can still name children and grandchildren. [[claim:lva-heirs]]
Claimants proved service with discharge papers or affidavits from officers and fellow soldiers. [[claim:lva-proof]]
Virginia bounty land lay in present-day Kentucky or Ohio. [[claim:lva-ky-oh]]
Warrants later surrendered for federal scrip are held by the National Archives with their application records. [[claim:lva-scrip]]

## How to use a bounty-land file in an application

1. Use it for service. Cite the file and the page that states the service.
2. Use it for family only where an heir or widow states the relationship.
3. Do not use the land location alone as residence. Pair it with tax, deed or census records from the same place.
4. Log any mismatch between the land and the family's known home as a conflict in the Lineage Evidence Matrix, then resolve it.
