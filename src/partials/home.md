This site helps you organize the records that connect you to a Revolutionary War patriot, see where your documented family line still has gaps, and find where to look for the missing proof. (A **patriot** here means someone who supported the cause of American independence, for example by fighting in its battles or serving in the Continental Congress.) [[claim:sar-about-patriots]]

Somewhere in your family's past, there may be someone who stood up for American independence. Maybe they carried a musket as a soldier, or maybe they helped in another way, such as furnishing supplies or lending money. [[claim:dar-material-aid]] If you'd like to honor that person by joining the Daughters or Sons of the American Revolution, you'll be asked to show, with records, how you are connected to them, one generation at a time. [[claim:dar-each-relationship]] [[claim:dar-join-docs]]

That can be harder than it sounds. The records are often in more than one place: for militia service, for example, the National Archives sends you to the archives of the state where the patriot served. [[claim:nara-svc-state]] Even a single pension file can arrive with its papers in no particular order. [[claim:nps-order]] And nobody hands you a map of which papers count as proof, or of what to try when the one record you need seems to have vanished.

Patriot, In Lay Terms explains the process in everyday language, the way a patient friend who has done this before might explain it at the kitchen table. The guides walk you through the kinds of records you're likely to find, what each one can show, and where to turn when something is missing. Two free tools help you lay out what you already have. The **Widow Pension Law Checker** shows which federal widow pension laws a couple's marriage fits and where her pension file may be. The **Lineage Evidence Matrix** lists, generation by generation, what is still missing in your line and where to look for it. Every fact on the site links to an official source, so you can check it yourself.

This site can't do the searching for you, and it won't pretend to. You'll still visit archives, courthouses and websites to find and copy the records themselves. A DAR member helps guide you through completing and submitting the application. [[claim:dar-member-guides]] SAR's local sponsor can guide you through the application. [[claim:sar-registrar-guide]] [[claim:sar-sponsor-registrar]] The society, not this site, reviews what you send: at DAR, all documentation is subject to review by its genealogy staff. [[claim:dar-review-staff]] What this site does is explain what each of those steps asks for and which records can answer it.

It's free, there's nothing to sign up for, and what you type into the tools stays in your own browser.
